# Chapter 3: Family-Business Persistence — The Hoshi Ryokan Case

## Purpose

Second worked example. Different institutional shape from Chapter 2 — a private family firm, not a multi-state treaty. The Hoshi Ryokan (Komatsu, Ishikawa Prefecture, Japan) has operated in the same family since 718 AD; the current operator is the 46th generation. Use the case to extend the four-conditions framework into a non-formal, non-state domain.

Key argumentative work:

- Show the framework adapting across institutional shapes (not state-bound)
- Surface what "distributed legitimacy" means when the institution is a family
- Engage the statistical question — how rare is this, really? — with care
- Set up Chapters 4 and 5 (religious order, constitutional republic) as further domain extensions
