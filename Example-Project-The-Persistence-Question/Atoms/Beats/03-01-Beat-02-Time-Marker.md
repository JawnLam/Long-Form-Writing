# Beat 2: Time Marker — 718 AD

## Purpose

Land the founding date. Do it carefully — the date carries enormous weight for the chapter and the book, but if the prose oversells, the reader checks out as if reading a tourist brochure. The beat lands the date as a fact, contextualizes its weight through the rotating-scroll detail (an artifact older than most countries), and introduces the Taichō dream-vision founding narrative without endorsing or dismissing it.

The beat should feel quiet. The weight of "1,300 years" should land through implication, not declamation.

## Content

*Paragraphs 4 and 5 of Section 3.1 (see [[03-01-Hoshi-Opening]] for the drafted prose).*

Anchors:

- The genkan and inner reception room
- The rotating scroll (quarterly rotation through twenty held in family archive; oldest from the seventeenth century)
- The hot spring underground pipe; the spring's pre-inn historical use
- The Taichō dream-vision founding narrative
- The methodological move: *"This is the story the family tells. It is also the story the family has been telling for a very long time."*

## Connects

- **Follows from:** [[03-01-Beat-01-Arrival]] — the sensory anchor
- **Leads to:** [[03-01-Beat-03-The-Family]] — the family

## Notes

- The methodological move (*"the story the family tells... has been telling for a very long time"*) is doing real work. It honors the tradition while signaling the writer's epistemological stance (the date is *the family's claim*, not asserted as historical bedrock). Voice samples support this kind of careful framing. Keep.
- The rotating-scroll detail is the first explicit adaptive-ritual artifact in the book (Chapter 2's adaptive-ritual examples were institutional rather than physical). Beat 2 is the first place where adaptive-ritual lands sensorily. Important to surface but don't *name* the thread yet — the beat works because the reader feels it without being told.
- "Twenty held in family archive" — verify this number with [[Hoshi-Ryokan-Records]] before locking. May be approximate; the manuscript needs a specific verified number or a clear acknowledgment of approximation.
- The Taichō-dream narrative is well-attested in family records and traditional accounts. The beat treats it as the traditional story rather than as historical fact — methodologically appropriate. Footnote during accuracy pass.
- The beat ends with the assertion that *"an inn has stood at this location and has been operated by descendants of one family across an unbroken chain of generations for more than a millennium."* This is the chapter's central factual claim. Verify with primary sources before the manuscript ships.
