# Beat 1: Arrival at the Ryokan

## Purpose

Open the chapter sensorily. Anchor the reader in a specific real place before any abstract argument lands. This beat does no analytical work; its job is to make the inn real to the reader, by description, before the chapter's analytical claims begin.

The beat should land present-tense — the inn is *there*, not *was there*. Voice samples confirm present-tense for opening narrative scenes.

## Content

*The first three paragraphs of Section 3.1 (see [[03-01-Hoshi-Opening]] for the drafted prose).*

The arrival scene: the road through Komatsu, the modern provincial buildings on either side, the bend in the road, the wooden inn with its tiled roof, the bridge over the stream, the pine tree, the wooden sign with the Hoshi characters.

Sensory anchors: the wood-tile texture, the pruned pine, the silence of the genkan, the polished floor.

## Notes

- The beat is currently drafted. Word count ~350. May tighten to ~280 in the prose-line revision pass; some of the modern-buildings detail can be cut without losing the contrast.
- The pruned pine is a load-bearing detail. It carries adaptive-ritual implicitly (the pruning is recent; the tradition is old) without naming the thread. Keep it.
- The wooden sign with the Hoshi characters specifically *carved at this entrance, by tradition, since the building was rebuilt after a fire in 1872* — this detail does multiple things: it surfaces a near-death moment (the 1872 fire), introduces "tradition" as a quiet first appearance of the adaptive-ritual thread, and establishes the inn's continuity through a small visible artifact. Keep all three.
