# Why Nations Fail: The Origins of Power, Prosperity, and Poverty

## Citation

Acemoglu, Daron, and James A. Robinson. *Why Nations Fail: The Origins of Power, Prosperity, and Poverty*. New York: Crown Business, 2012.

## Why it matters here

Acemoglu and Robinson propose that nations succeed or fail based on whether their political and economic institutions are *inclusive* (broad participation, distributed power, secure property rights) or *extractive* (narrow elite control, weak property rights, extractive economic relations).

This is *close to but not the same as* the book's distributed-legitimacy thread. Both arguments are about how power is structured. But Acemoglu-Robinson's question is about *prosperity* (why nations are rich or poor); this book's question is about *persistence* (why institutions last or don't). Related but distinct.

The book engages Acemoglu-Robinson primarily to:

1. Distinguish persistence from prosperity. Some persistent institutions are inclusive; some are extractive. The framework needs to allow for this.
2. Borrow the methodological seriousness of their structural-comparative approach.

## Key claims / passages

- **Chapter 3** — the institutional framework. Read for the inclusive/extractive distinction.
- **Chapter 4** — long-run comparative evidence (Nogales, Korea, etc.). Useful comparative-method model.
- **Chapter 12, pp. 368–402** — extractive institutions can persist via "creative destruction" suppression; relevant for the book's North Korea and Soviet Union counter-cases in Chapter 6.

## Worries

- Acemoglu and Robinson have been heavily criticized by some economists (e.g., Bill Easterly) for selection bias in their cases. The book should engage the framework without endorsing it as settled.
- The "inclusive vs. extractive" binary is too coarse for the book's framework. A more nuanced distributed-legitimacy treatment is needed; Acemoglu-Robinson is starting point, not destination.
- The 2024 Nobel Prize to Acemoglu, Robinson, and Simon Johnson means *Why Nations Fail* has new attention. Be careful not to position the book *against* a Nobel-laureated framework without serious engagement. The book's argument is *complementary* to theirs, not oppositional.
