## Patterns observed this cartridge

### 2026-06-02 — soft-close-on-example

**Pattern:** Sections end on the concrete example without landing the closing claim that ties the example to the chapter's argument. The example is vivid; the prose stops; the reader is left to infer the load-bearing point.

**Instances in this cartridge:**

- [[03-01-Hoshi-Opening]] Beat 3 — closes on "across a span of time during which every other institution any reader of this book belongs to has come into existence, often several times over." Beautiful sentence; doesn't quite land what the chapter will claim about the Hoshi case.
- [[02-03-The-Treaty-Today]] (drafted, per state) — per session notes from 2026-05-30, the section's closing paragraph leaves the chapter's argumentative move implicit
- (One earlier instance flagged in Session 010 according to `_state.md` Recent Sessions narrative — likely Chapter 1's Section 02)

**Proposed fix or drill:** Before closing a section that ends on an example, write the closing-claim sentence first, then place the example before it. The example becomes the setup; the claim becomes the landing. Practice for next 3 sections.

**Status:** active — proposed at this session's CRAFT-REVIEW; writer to confirm

### 2026-06-02 — em-dash cadence dependency

**Pattern:** Sentences with high information density default to em-dash structures more than the prose can support without monotony. The em-dash is the writer's strongest rhythm tool (per voice samples) but at 2.3 em-dashes per long paragraph the rhythm becomes its own background noise.

**Instances in this cartridge:**

- Voice sample 1 (the institutional-resilience essay fragment) — 5 em-dashes in 8 sentences
- [[03-01-Hoshi-Opening]] Beat 3 — 4 em-dashes in the closing paragraph

**Proposed fix or drill:** During REVISE on any drafted section, count em-dashes per paragraph. Where >2, force one to become a colon or a sentence break; see if the rhythm survives. Don't replace all of them (the writer's voice depends on them) — just thin the rhythm.

**Status:** active — proposed; this one may be a voice feature rather than a pattern to correct, depending on the writer's call during CRAFT-REVIEW
