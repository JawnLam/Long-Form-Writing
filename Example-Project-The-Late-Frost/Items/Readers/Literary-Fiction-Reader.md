# Reader — Literary Fiction Reader

## Who they are

A literate, well-read reader who reads ~25 books a year, ~70% literary fiction. Subscribes to *The New Yorker*. Has read Patchett, Bennett, Hadley, O'Farrell, Lahiri, Strout. Reads slowly and re-reads sentences. Will put a novel down if it loses them and may or may not pick it up again.

## What they want from this novel

- Prose that earns its sentences. Each paragraph should do work.
- Characters whose interiority is specific, not generic. They want to know which sister this is from the first sentence of each chapter, not because the prose announces it but because the sentence-shape is hers.
- A literary pacing — not thriller-momentum but a sense that the novel is going somewhere. Three weeks compressed into 75,000 words has to feel like time is doing something, not just elapsing.
- A central question whose answer matters and whose unfolding is not over-explained.
- An ending that earns its openness. They will tolerate ambiguity; they will not tolerate the writer evading the central question's pressure.

## What they fear in this novel

- Sentimentality at the sister-reconciliation moments — they have read enough family-fiction to detect when warmth is *bought* rather than *earned*. They will close the book at the first unearned warmth.
- Vineyard-procedural padding — they did not pick this up to learn about wine-making. The setting must do work for the story, not the other way around.
- The dead father becoming a metaphor too easily. They want him to remain a person with specific habits and specific failures, not a symbol of the past.

## Simulation prompts for the AI

When asked to do READER-SIMULATION with this reader (chapter 12 §5 / fiction reframe):

- Track *what is the reader registering this paragraph?* — not just plot information, but emotional weight, tonal register, character-specific cues
- Track *where would this reader put the book down?* — boredom, distrust, distance, sentimentality
- Track *what is the reader investing in?* — which question, which character, which outcome are they leaning toward by the end of each chapter

## Notes

- This reader's primary register is *what does the prose earn*. The AI's reader-simulation should not slide into plot-summary; it should track the moment-to-moment perceptual experience.
- This reader is forgiving of slow opening if the prose carries weight. Not forgiving of slow opening if the prose is loose.
