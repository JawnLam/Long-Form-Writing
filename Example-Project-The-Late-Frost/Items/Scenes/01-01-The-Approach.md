# 01-01 — The Approach

## Setting and Stakes

Late afternoon, April 4. Maya drives north on Highway 12 from SFO, the rental car's cabin smelling of new vinyl. The Mayacamas to the east, the Sonoma Mountains to the west. Late-light through the gaps. The stakes are quiet: Maya thinks she is making a brief visit to handle paperwork and the funeral-aftermath logistics. POV is Maya's close-third, present-tense.

## Value-shift *(v1.2)*

- **Whose want drives this scene:** Maya wants to arrive at the vineyard, do what needs doing tonight (settle in, eat something, sleep), and begin tomorrow's paperwork with the lawyer in the morning. She wants the visit to be small.
- **What's the conflict:** The vineyard refuses to be small. The frost-damage on the neighbor's vines, the temperature of the air, the empty house, the moved-in absence of her father — these refuse the size Maya wants the visit to have.
- **Start-state (from):** *ignorant* — Maya does not know about the debts; does not know Sarah knows; expects routine logistics; does not yet register the frost-threat as more than a passing weather note
- **End-state (to):** *suspecting* — Maya does not have any specific knowledge yet, but the texture of the house, the unopened letter from the lawyer, and the absent-but-occupied feel of the kitchen have moved her into a posture of attention. Something is off. She does not know what.
- **The turn — what changes:** Maya arrives expecting administrative weight and discovers atmospheric weight she cannot yet name. The unopened letter is the visible fulcrum but the turn is internal — Maya's nervous system has rotated 30 degrees.
- **But / Therefore connector to the next scene:** *therefore.* The unease moves Maya toward Sarah's arrival with new attention. The first dinner (Scene 2-2) lands on different soil because of this turn.
