# Motif — The Empty Chair

## What this motif is

The chair at the head of the dining table. Daniel Hollis sat in it for every meal of his life from age 28 to age 71. After his death, the chair has been empty in two different ways:

1. *Empty* — nobody sits in it
2. *Empty in a specific way* — pulled out an inch, at a slight angle, as if someone (Sarah, during her summer-before-Daniel's-death visit; Hector, possibly, the morning after Daniel died) sat in it briefly and left it that way and nobody since has pushed it back

The motif is the *specificity* of the empty chair. Not a generic absent-father object. A particular chair, particularly positioned, particularly noticed.

## Why this motif

- The father's absence is the novel's deep ground; the motif provides a specific physical fulcrum for that absence
- The chair's *position* is the carrier of information — pulled out and at an angle is the sign that someone has been here, which is the sensory ground for Maya's suspicion-state at the close of Scene 1-1 (per SCENE-AUDIT flag, this is the candidate for Beat 4's value-shift fulcrum)
- The chair becomes a witnessing object across the three weeks; what changes about it (someone pushes it in; someone sits in it; someone moves it) becomes the silent register of the relationship's reorganization

## Image-cluster vocabulary (for prose consistency)

- *Pulled out* (the chair's position descriptor — must remain this specific phrase)
- *Head of the table*
- *Pushed in* / *left out*
- *Angle*, *slight*, *inch* (the specific geometry)
- Avoid: any naming of the chair as symbolic; any character ever mentioning that the chair *means* anything

## What it must NOT do

- The motif must remain physical. No character ever calls it "the empty chair." The phrase appears only in the writer's notes and in the title of this Item.
- The chair must not become a recurring metaphor flashing on too often. Roughly six appearances across the novel (per the table above). Each appearance does specific work.
- The chair must not be cleared up too tidily. The Chapter 14 push-in is not a resolution; it is a small physical act by one specific sister, in a specific moment, for specific reasons. The reader's interpretation is left open.
