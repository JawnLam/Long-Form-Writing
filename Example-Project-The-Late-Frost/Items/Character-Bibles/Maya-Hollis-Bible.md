# Maya Hollis — Bible

> **First-pass bible for the protagonist. Created in session 004 as the worked v1.3.1 demonstration. Bible is operator-private in the writer's own work; included in this shipped example via the `!Example-Project-*` override. Gaps are acceptable at this status (`drafting`).**

## At-a-glance

Maya Hollis, 41, an architect in Portland with a husband and a nine-year-old daughter. The older Hollis sister by three years. Returns to the Sonoma vineyard nine months after her father's death to handle the estate and discovers, over three weeks of frost-watch, that her sister Sarah has been carrying the secret of their father's debts for three months. A manager by disposition; the novel teaches her the difference between managing and being honest. Plain-spoken; architectural eye; calculates and catches herself calculating.

## Physical

- 5'7", lean, runs three mornings a week in Portland and keeps it up at the vineyard with the dawn frost-watch walks
- Hair dark brown shoulder-length, often up in a clip when she's working; goes grey at the temples (early; both parents went grey early)
- Eyes: hazel, almost green in some light; the eye-color is one of the things Sarah used to comment on when they were children and stopped commenting on after their mother died
- Posture: erect, slightly forward-leaning. Reads as competence. Architect-trained — she stands the way the spaces she designs ask people to stand
- Wardrobe: practical professional. Linen and wool. A wristwatch, never a smartwatch. A wedding ring she sometimes turns absently when she's thinking
- Hands: long fingers, short nails, a small scar on her left index finger from a knife slip when she was twelve. The scar is a Maya-detail Sarah will notice in a Chapter 7 sequel-beat

## Backstory (chronological)

- **Born:** December 1984, San Francisco; the Hollis family moved to the Sonoma vineyard when Maya was 18 months old
- **1989:** Sarah is born
- **1998 (age 14):** Their mother dies of cancer over an eight-week summer. Maya is at the bedside for the last three weeks. Sarah is at a neighbor's house being shielded. Maya does not forgive herself for this protection-by-shielding; she will spend her life unable to name why. *(See Wounds.)*
- **1999–2002:** High school in Sonoma. Maya runs cross-country, takes AP everything, is the daughter the school principal points to when prospective parents tour the school
- **2002:** Berkeley undergrad. Architecture
- **2006:** Berkeley → University of Oregon (master's). Why Oregon: distance from the vineyard
- **2009:** Hired at a Portland firm specializing in adaptive-reuse
- **2011:** Meets Ben, an environmental lawyer, at a dinner party. They date for two years
- **2014:** Marries Ben. Sarah comes for the wedding; their first significant time together since 2002
- **2016:** Iris is born. Sarah is in Brooklyn; sends a card; doesn't visit for four months
- **2019:** Maya's father, Daniel, has a heart attack scare; angioplasty; recovers. Maya visits for a weekend. The first long conversation with Daniel about the vineyard's finances (now in retrospect: he had begun borrowing then; he did not tell her)
- **2022:** Maya's last visit to the vineyard before Daniel's death. Three days, with Iris. Sarah is not there
- **2025 (July, ~9 months before the novel opens):** Daniel dies of a second heart attack. Maya flies down, manages the funeral and the immediate estate. Sarah comes for two days. They speak almost not at all
- **2025 (August–March 2026):** Maya manages the estate's open items from Portland. Frank Mendel sends both sisters letters in January. Maya's copy was sent to the vineyard, where it sat unopened until day 1 of the novel
- **2026 (April 4):** The novel opens. Maya drives north from SFO

## Family / lineage / ancestry

- **Daniel Hollis** (father, 1954–2025) — born and raised in Sonoma; family vineyard since 1968. Took over from his father in 1992. Quiet, withholding, showed affection through work. Long widowhood. Closer to Sarah than to Maya — a fact Maya has registered without articulating
- **Helen Marks Hollis** (mother, 1956–1998) — born in San Francisco; a high school teacher before marrying Daniel; the warmth in the family
- **Sarah Hollis** (sister, b. 1989) — see [[Sarah-Hollis]]
- **Maya's husband Ben Cavalcanti** (b. 1980) — environmental lawyer in Portland; not Hollis-family; the off-stage marriage that grounds Maya
- **Iris Cavalcanti-Hollis** (daughter, b. 2016) — the third generation; Maya's deepest fear is failing her

## Worldview

- **Core beliefs (load-bearing):**
  - Spaces shape behavior; if you want people to be different, change what they live in
  - Honesty is a discipline, not a virtue
  - The older sister's job is to carry weather so the younger sister doesn't have to *(this is the lie she believes — the novel teaches her it's the lie)*
- **Held-lightly:**
  - Mild liberal politics, unexamined
  - Religion: agnostic; raised loosely Methodist; doesn't go
- **What she thinks the world is fundamentally like:**
  - The world is structured. The structure can be read and worked with. People who fail in the world have usually misread the structure. (This is part of why she became an architect — and part of what the novel will expose as protective fiction.)

## Habits and routines

- Up at 5:45. Coffee black, made by hand-grind. Run at 6. Shower. Work by 8
- Calls Iris every night when traveling, 7pm Pacific. Calls Ben after Iris's bedtime
- Reads in bed for 25 minutes before sleep — currently halfway through a Jenny Erpenbeck novel
- Cleans as she cooks. Does the dishes immediately after dinner. This is a small consistency that the novel will use as a Sarah-noticing-it moment (Chapter 4)
- Sundays: takes Iris to a Portland coffee shop where Iris draws and Maya reads the paper. The Sunday-with-Iris ritual is in three weeks of jeopardy during the novel

## Skills, knowledge, and competence

- **Expert at:** spatial reasoning, building-code knowledge, adaptive-reuse design, client management
- **Competent at:** wine-country logistics (grew up there), domestic cooking, distance running
- **Publicly thought good vs actually good:** publicly thought of as the family-stabilizer; actually as fragile in that role as anyone else but has never let it show
- **Manuscript-relevant:** the architectural eye is the prose's primary perceptual mode for Maya's POV chapters

## Wounds (deep)

- **The protection-by-shielding wound (1998):** Maya was 14 when their mother died over an eight-week summer. The adult family decided (without explicit conversation; through tacit agreement) that Sarah, at 11, would be shielded from the deathbed. Maya was kept close. After her mother died, Maya stepped into the parenting role for Sarah that the family expected. Maya has spent twenty-eight years believing this was the right and necessary thing. Sarah has spent the same twenty-eight years half-resenting it without being able to name why. The novel will surface this in Chapter 10. The wound is structural; the protection caused the estrangement
- **The father's silence wound:** Daniel never said *good job* to Maya. She has carried this without articulating it. Sarah, who had a different relationship with the father, knew he didn't say it to anyone — but Maya never asked Sarah, and Sarah never told her
- **The maternal wound:** Helen's last words to Maya at 14 were *take care of your sister.* Maya took this literally for the next twenty-eight years. The novel will reveal she took it more literally than Helen would have wanted

## Secrets

- **Nothing from Sarah at the novel's opening.** Maya is the sister without secrets in this novel; the secrets are Sarah's. This is itself information: Maya's openness is part of why the imbalance feels intolerable when she discovers it
- **A small thing from Ben:** Maya was offered a partner-track position at her firm six months ago; she's been waiting to tell Ben until she decides whether to accept. The deferral has lasted longer than it should. This will surface in a Chapter 7 or 9 conversation with Sarah, when Sarah asks (correctly) why Maya hasn't told Ben

## Contradictions

- Believes she is open and honest; defers the partner-track conversation with Ben for six months
- Believes she let Sarah grow up shielded; in fact the shielding *itself* was the not-letting-grow-up
- Believes she has no relationship to the vineyard; the vineyard's gravity on her three days into the novel will expose this as protective fiction
- Values plain-spokenness; says "I see" as a placeholder when she does not see
- The deepest contradiction: Maya is, in her own self-account, the responsible one. The novel's actual subject is what that self-account has cost her

## Arc across this manuscript

- **Opening:** dutiful, distant, expecting to manage the estate and leave (Chapter 1)
- **First turn:** the empty house, the moved letter, the unspoken weight — suspecting (end of Chapter 1)
- **Second turn:** the work of the first cold night opens an unexpected connection with Sarah — *cautiously bonded* (Chapter 3)
- **Discovery:** reads the letter; learns Sarah knew (Chapter 5)
- **Confrontation:** says the thing in dialogue; Sarah's defense lands and doesn't (Chapter 5–6)
- **The middle:** has to choose whether to continue working with Sarah under the new condition; chooses to — pragmatically at first, then honestly (Chapter 7–9)
- **Crisis:** the fight about the father (or its displacement; Chapter 10). Maya cries for the first time in the book, while continuing to argue
- **Resolution:** at the close, Maya is the sister who *speaks* — she is the one who, in Chapter 13, says the thing that closes the central question. The architectural-management self-account is gone; what's left is harder
- **Final state:** Maya does not have the answer the novel began promising. She has a *different* relationship to the vineyard, to Sarah, and to the question of what to do about the partner-track offer (and Ben). The novel ends without resolving any of these except the relationship with Sarah, which is now what it is

## Relationships

### With [[Sarah-Hollis]]

- **History:** see Wounds. Twenty-eight years of structurally distorted older-sister role. Three years of explicit estrangement
- **Current state (Day 1):** courteous, careful, distance maintained
- **Evolution:** opens during the first frost-night work (Chapter 3); cracks at the letter (Chapter 5); restructures across Chapters 6–10; settles into the new shape in Chapters 11–14
- **Subtext:** Maya is more relieved by Sarah's company than she's let herself admit. Maya's wariness about Sarah's withholding is real but also a protective frame over the relief

### With Ben (husband)

- **History:** thirteen-year marriage, twelve years parenting Iris together
- **Current state:** stable, distant during the novel because the novel takes place at the vineyard
- **Evolution:** off-stage; the partner-track deferral will surface
- **Subtext:** Ben is the marriage that grounds Maya; Maya is the marriage Ben married into without expecting it to require his patience this much

### With Iris (daughter)

- The thing Maya is most afraid of failing. Three calls during the three weeks. Iris draws pictures of vines. The drawings will surface in a Chapter 9 sequel-beat

### With Hector (vineyard manager)

- Polite, somewhat distant, with twenty years of texture underneath
- Hector knows things Maya doesn't (the unpaid wages, the Sarah-summer visit). The relationship will change when Maya learns

### With Daniel (father, deceased)

- The dead-father relationship is one Maya is still in. The Chapter 10 fight will be partly about this

## Voice (extended)

*Building on the Character Item's voice-and-prose-register and dialogue-tells sections.*

- **Pet phrases not yet appearing in scenes:** *(reserved for first appearance)*: "I'm not asking you" (at a confrontation moment in Chapter 5 or 6)
- **Verbal register at different ages:** Maya at 14 had a more colloquial register than current Maya. The current Maya's plain-architectural-prose is itself a structure she built after her mother's death; the brief flashes of younger-Maya in Chapter 10 (during the fight about Helen's death) should sound different
- **How voice shifts under stress:** Sentences shorten. Adverbs drop. The architectural noticing intensifies and then disappears entirely. The disappearance of the architectural noticing IS the highest-pressure tell

## Sensory signatures

- **Smell:** soap and clean linen. A coffee-and-ink scent in the morning. No perfume
- **Sound of her walk:** quiet, even, slightly faster than most people. The first day at the vineyard she walks slower; the prose will register this
- **The way light hits her:** Maya looks like a photograph in late-afternoon light. (Sarah, who is a film editor, will register this in a Chapter 2 sequel-beat — the cinematographer's eye seeing her sister as a frame)
- **Her handwriting:** architectural. All-caps. Precise. The reader sees this in a Chapter 11 scene when she leaves Sarah a note
