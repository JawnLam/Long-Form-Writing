# Chapter 1 — Maya Arrives at the Vineyard

## Purpose in the manuscript

Open the novel. Get Maya to the vineyard alone. Establish the empty-house tone and the absent father. Plant the first frost-warning (Promise 1) via the visible damage on a neighbor's vines. Plant the unopened lawyer's letter (Promise 2) on the kitchen counter. Establish Maya's POV register — close-third, present-tense, plain sentences for observation, layered for interiority.

## Scenes in this chapter

1. [[01-01-The-Approach]] — Maya drives up Highway 12 from SFO; sees frost-damage on a neighbor's vines; arrives at the vineyard alone; walks through the empty house; finds the unopened letter on the kitchen counter

*(Current outline is a single long scene for Chapter 1. If during drafting the scene runs significantly long, it splits into 1-1 The Approach and 1-2 The House, with the door as the cleave.)*

## Chapter-level value-arc

- **Opening state:** Maya is returning to a place she has not been in three years; she expects to do practical paperwork and leave. Her relationship to the vineyard is dutiful.
- **Closing state:** Maya is no longer sure the visit is going to be brief or practical. She has not opened the letter but she has registered it. The vineyard is not the museum-piece she expected.
- **Value-shift across the chapter:** dutiful-distance → unsettled-present

## Motifs engaged

- [[The-Late-Frost]] — primary, surfaced via the neighbor's damaged vines and Maya's awareness that her own vines are vulnerable
- [[The-Empty-Chair]] — latent; passed through but not yet weight-bearing (the formal first appearance comes in Chapter 2's first dinner scene)

## Characters present

- [[Maya-Hollis]] (POV)
- [[Sarah-Hollis]] (absent but referenced — Sarah's flight has not landed yet)
- [[Hector-Reyes]] (absent — he is at his own house on the property; Maya does not seek him out tonight; first appearance in Chapter 2)

## Notes for revision

- Chapter 1 is the slowest in the book; this is structurally correct (Williams: "open slow, accelerate") but the slowness must remain felt-present, not merely descriptive
- Watch the temptation to over-explain the family backstory in the first chapter; it can be parceled out
- The letter's planting must feel like an observation, not an announcement. The reader should register it almost in peripheral vision

- **Pays off:** nothing (opening chapter)
- **Parallels:** Chapter 14's morning (one sister speaks) — the empty house at the opening and the changed house at the close
