# Maya Hollis

## Function in this manuscript

Maya is the protagonist and the POV of odd-numbered chapters. She is the sister who *does not* know about the debts at the novel's opening; her arc traces the discovery, the confrontation, and the reorganization of her relationship to Sarah, to the vineyard, and to the dead father whose withholding she will discover was structural.

## Demographics

- **Age:** 41 at the novel's opening
- **Location:** Lives in Portland, Oregon. Has lived there since age 27.
- **Profession:** Architect. Mid-career, at a mid-sized firm specializing in adaptive-reuse projects (warehouses to housing; churches to community spaces). Works in spaces other people built and inherited.
- **Family:** Married to Ben, an environmental lawyer; one daughter, Iris, age 9. Ben and Iris are not at the vineyard during the novel; they call.
- **Education:** Berkeley undergrad, then a master's in architecture from Oregon.

## Want / Need / Wound

- **Want (external, scene-level):** to handle the inherited vineyard responsibly — sell it cleanly, settle the estate, return home to Portland by month's end
- **Need (internal, novel-level):** to stop being the older sister who manages from a distance; to stop confusing "managing" with "loving"
- **Wound:** When the sisters' mother died (Maya was 14, Sarah was 11), Maya stepped into a parenting role for Sarah that she has never fully stepped out of. She believes — without ever having articulated it — that her job in any sister-relationship is to manage Sarah's emotional weather rather than to share weather with her.
- **Lie she believes:** that being responsible *for* Sarah is the same as being honest *with* Sarah

## Voice and prose register (close-third POV)

- **Sentence shape:** Plain, observational, with one short-layered passage per beat when interiority surfaces
- **Diction:** Architectural, spatial — Maya notices rooms, surfaces, light, the way furniture is arranged. The vineyard becomes a kind of building to her
- **Habits of mind:** Calculates. Catches herself calculating. Resists the calculation but does not stop it
- **Pet phrases / verbal tics:** "all right" as a sentence ("All right.") at moments of decision. "I see" as a placeholder when she does not yet see

### Dialogue tells *(v1.3.1)*

- **Sentence shape:** Short, declarative. Often fragmentary in moments of decision.
- **Diction range:** Plain. Slight register-shifting downward toward Hector (more equal, less manager-shaped). No shift around Sarah — they speak the shorthand of a long shared life.
- **Pet phrases:** "All right." as a sentence. "I see" as a placeholder. "Fine." as an end-of-conversation marker.
- **Verbal tics:** Says the listener's name when she's about to say something hard ("Sarah —")
- **What she says when she doesn't know what to say:** "I see." Followed by silence. The silence is the answer.
- **What she says when she's lying:** Goes more precise. Adds a detail that isn't necessary. (The tell: necessary detail in a lying-Maya sentence is the same shape as decorative detail in a truthful-Maya sentence; only the precision-density shifts.)
- **What she says under pressure:** Register doesn't escalate. Sentences get shorter. Cuts adverbs. The compression IS the pressure.

## Subtext patterns *(v1.3.1)*

Maya speaks plainly when the topic is *external* (the vineyard, the work, the weather). She speaks around when the topic is *interior* (the relationship, the father, the past). The reader will register the pattern: short plain sentences about cabernet block frost defense; sentences-with-evasions about Sarah's three-year silence.

The most reliable subtext-mechanism in Maya's dialogue: she answers the *previous* question. Sarah asks something hard; Maya answers something else from earlier in the conversation. The reader hears the gap; Sarah hears it too but does not name it.

## Relationships

- **Sarah (sister):** Three years of estrangement. The estrangement was not a fight; it was a slow drift after Daniel's diagnosis became serious. Maya thought the drift was Sarah's fault. The novel will reveal it was structural.
- **Daniel (father, deceased):** Loved him from a distance; was not close. Suspected he favored Sarah but never tested the suspicion.
- **Hector Reyes (vineyard manager):** Has not seen Hector in three years. The relationship is dutiful, warm at the surface.
- **Ben (husband):** Off-stage. Stable. Calls every evening. Their marriage is not the novel's subject.
- **Iris (daughter):** Off-stage. The thing Maya is most afraid of failing is Iris; the vineyard's claim on her time during the frost-watch will surface this.

## What's changing for them in this manuscript

- The discovery that Sarah has been carrying the debt-secret for three months will force Maya to recognize that her older-sister-management has cost her the very thing it was trying to protect
- The frost-watch's physical labor will give Maya something to do other than manage
- The vineyard will become, briefly, a thing she sees rather than a thing she manages

## Behavioral consistency check

- Maya does not raise her voice. Even in the Chapter 5 confrontation, she does not raise her voice. The dialogue lands harder for that.
- Maya does not cry until late. Specifically, not in Chapter 1, not at the confrontation, not at the morning-after. She cries in Chapter 10 during the fight about the father (or its displacement) — once, briefly, while continuing to argue.
- Maya does the dishes. This is a small consistency tell. She does the dishes in every domestic scene. She does them efficiently. Sarah notices and minds.
