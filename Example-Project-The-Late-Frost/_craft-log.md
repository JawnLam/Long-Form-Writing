## Decisions logged

### 2026-06-02 — Decision: POV alternation by chapter, not by scene

**Decided:** POV alternates by chapter (Maya odd, Sarah even). Not by scene within a chapter. Not by paragraph.

**Why:** Per-scene alternation would dissipate the information-asymmetry tension. The reader needs sustained time inside one sister to register the unspoken weight of what they're not saying.

**How to apply:** No scene within a chapter takes the other sister's POV. If a scene structurally needs the other sister's POV, the scene belongs in the other chapter.

### 2026-06-02 — Decision: Present tense

**Decided:** Present tense for the entire novel.

**Why:** The novel's central tension is "what one sister might say or do at any moment now." Past tense distances that pressure. Present tense holds the reader inside the unbearable now.

**How to apply:** Resist the impulse to slip into past tense for backstory passages. Backstory enters as present-tense memory ("Maya thinks of the summer their mother died") not as past-tense narration ("That summer, their mother died").

### 2026-06-02 — Decision: Sister dialogue plain, interiority layered

**Decided:** The sisters' dialogue is plain, short, often unfinished. Their interior monologues are more layered.

**Why:** The sisters grew up together; they speak the shorthand of long shared life. The interior is where their separate years live.

**How to apply:** Resist the impulse to make the dialogue elegant. Resist the impulse to give the interiority crisp closure.

## Things to watch in this manuscript

- **Sentimentality risk in sister-reconciliation moments.** Sister-relationship fiction can tip into sentimentality at the moments where the relationship softens. The writer wants to resist this. Specifically: every moment of warmth between Maya and Sarah should remain edged, contingent, costly — not resolved.
- **Vineyard-procedural risk.** The setting includes machinery and craft (wind machines, smudge pots, bud-break biology). Too much of this turns the literary novel into a procedural. Calibration: enough detail to ground the reader; not enough to slow the relationship engine.
- **Father-grief on-the-nose risk.** The dead father is structurally central but the writer is wary of writing "the dead father" scene. Per `_outline.md` Chapter 10 note: the father-fight may be displaced into a fight about Hector's compensation, with the father as the absence in the room. Decision deferred.
- **Maya-Sarah voice differentiation in close-third POV.** The alternating-POV structure requires Maya's and Sarah's interiority to *feel* distinct without becoming caricatures of "the older protective one" vs "the younger withdrawn one." Watch.
