## Per-pair detail

### s1 — Maya ↔ Sarah (sisters)

- **Type:** sisters; older-younger by three years
- **History:** Childhood at the vineyard until Helen's death in 1998 (Maya 14, Sarah 11). Maya stepped into the parenting role; Sarah was structurally shielded. Twenty-eight years of distortion. Three years of explicit estrangement before the novel
- **Current state at manuscript opening:** courteous, careful, distance maintained. Three years of no significant contact except Daniel's funeral
- **Evolution arc across the manuscript:** Opens during first frost-night work (Chapter 3); cracks at the letter (Chapter 5); restructures across Chapters 6–10; settles into the new shape in Chapters 11–14
- **Subtext:** Maya is more relieved by Sarah's company than she's let herself admit. Sarah is more grateful for Maya's protection than she can name, and more resentful of the protection than she can name
- **Asymmetry:** [[Maya-Hollis]]'s Relationships section frames the estrangement as "slow drift after Daniel's diagnosis." [[Sarah-Hollis]]'s Relationships section frames it as "Sarah's choice, partially — believing she was giving Maya autonomy." The asymmetry is intentional and load-bearing; the novel surfaces both views

### s2 — Maya ↔ Hector

- **Type:** family employer / employee; twenty-year texture
- **History:** Hector hired 2009; Maya has lived in Portland since 2009. The relationship is built on annual three-day visits and Daniel's between-times reports about Hector
- **Current state:** polite, somewhat distant, with twenty years of texture underneath
- **Evolution:** opens during the first frost-night work (Maya sees Hector as a working professional rather than a family fixture); changes substantively when Maya learns Hector has not been fully paid for five months and when Maya learns Hector knew Sarah was at the vineyard the summer before Daniel died
- **Subtext:** Hector is loyal to the property more than to either sister; the novel does not resolve which sister he ultimately backs
- **Asymmetry:** Maya frames Hector as "the vineyard's manager"; Hector frames Maya as "the older daughter who lives in Portland." Both are accurate; both are insufficient

### s3 — Maya ↔ Daniel † (father, deceased)

- **Type:** father-daughter; nine months posthumous
- **History:** Maya loved him from a distance; was not close. Suspected he favored Sarah but never tested the suspicion. Daniel never said *good job* to Maya (Maya carries this without articulating)
- **Current state:** Daniel is dead. Maya is still in the relationship (per [[Maya-Hollis-Bible]] Arc — the dead-father relationship is one Maya is still in)
- **Evolution:** The Chapter 10 fight (or its displacement) will be partly about this. Maya will articulate something about her father that she has never said
- **Subtext:** Daniel's silence was structural; Maya's plain-spokenness was the structure she built in response. The Chapter 10 fight will expose this
- **Asymmetry:** n/a — Daniel is dead. The asymmetry is between Maya's frame of him and Sarah's

### s4 — Maya ↔ Helen † (mother, deceased)

- **Type:** mother-daughter; twenty-eight years posthumous
- **History:** Helen's last words to Maya at 14 were *take care of your sister.* Maya took this literally for the next twenty-eight years
- **Current state:** Maya rarely thinks of Helen directly; the *take-care-of-your-sister* injunction is operative without being thought
- **Evolution:** A Chapter 10 flashback (or memory-fragment) will surface Helen's actual words and Maya will recognize, for the first time, that *take care* was less literal than she made it
- **Subtext:** The injunction reshaped Maya's entire relationship to Sarah. Recognizing this is the novel's deepest emotional event for Maya

### s5 — Sarah ↔ Hector

- **Type:** family employer / employee; intermittent
- **History:** Hector knew Sarah primarily through Daniel's reports until 2024 summer (Sarah's two-month stay at the vineyard before Daniel died). Hector saw Sarah at her father's bedside; Hector has not told Maya this
- **Current state:** closer than Maya is to Hector; Sarah speaks more Spanish; Sarah is aware Hector has been carrying things for the family
- **Evolution:** Sarah may learn from Hector about his unpaid wages in Chapter 4 (decision deferred). The information-state ledger in [[_continuity]] tracks this
- **Subtext:** Hector and Sarah share the silence-keeping pattern Sarah inherited from Daniel. They recognize each other as silence-keepers
- **Asymmetry:** Sarah's [[Sarah-Hollis]] atom notes Hector "knew Sarah was at the vineyard the summer before Daniel died" as an information-state fact. Hector's frame on the relationship may surface in Chapter 8 or 10 conversations

### s6 — Sarah ↔ Daniel † (father, deceased)

- **Type:** father-daughter; nine months posthumous
- **History:** Closer than Maya was. Spent two months with him at the vineyard the summer before he died. Knows things about him Maya does not. Some of those things are about the debts; some are about other matters
- **Current state:** Sarah is also still in the relationship. Her closeness to him during his decline is the source of her information advantage about the debts
- **Evolution:** Sarah will recognize, during the novel, that the closeness she had with him was bought partly with a willingness to be his silence-keeper. The recognition lands in Chapter 10 or thereabouts
- **Subtext:** Sarah was the family member who inherited the silence-keeping pattern from Daniel. The novel will expose this

### s7 — Sarah ↔ Helen † (mother, deceased)

- **Type:** mother-daughter; truncated at age 11
- **History:** Sarah's memories of Helen are fragmentary. Helen was Sarah's safe person; Helen's death is Sarah's first wound
- **Current state:** Sarah occasionally thinks of Helen; Helen is the absence under all other absences
- **Evolution:** Sarah's memories may surface in fragments across her POV chapters. Decision: no flashback scene; only fragments

### s8 — Hector ↔ Daniel † (employer / employee; deceased)

- **Type:** professional with deep texture
- **History:** Hired 2009. Sixteen years working for Daniel. The closest non-family relationship in Daniel's life after Helen died
- **Current state:** Daniel is dead. Hector continues operations; Hector is owed wages. Hector grieves Daniel without sentimentality
- **Subtext:** Hector knew about the borrowing (he saw the financial pressure manifest in operational compromises across 2019–2025). Hector's silence on the debts to the sisters mirrors Daniel's silence. The pattern propagates

### s9 — Daniel † ↔ Helen † (deceased spouses)

- **Type:** marriage, 1980–1998
- **History:** Twelve years of marriage; two daughters; Helen's death at age 42 of aggressive cancer
- **Current state:** both deceased
- **Operative in the novel:** their relationship's *absence* shapes Daniel's twenty-seven-year widowhood and thus shapes both daughters

## Triangle dynamics

- **Maya ↔ Sarah ↔ Daniel:** the central triangle. The dead father's differential closeness to the two daughters is the structural wound the novel will address. Chapter 10 is where this lands
- **Maya ↔ Sarah ↔ Helen:** the prior triangle. The shielding-from-the-deathbed at 1998 is the originating event of the sister relationship's distortion
- **Sarah ↔ Hector ↔ Daniel:** the silence-keeping triangle. The information-state ledger ([[_continuity]]) tracks this triangle's information flow
