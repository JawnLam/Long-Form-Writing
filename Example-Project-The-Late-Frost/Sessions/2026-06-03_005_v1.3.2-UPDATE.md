## What we did

### `_worldbuilding.md` — minimal stub

Created `_worldbuilding.md` as a one-page placeholder documenting the choice *not* to expand it. The Late Frost is contemporary literary realism; substantive world-rules live in `_continuity.md` instead. This is the v1.3.2 worked-example demonstration of what a minimal worldbuilding file looks like for a cartridge that doesn't need substantive worldbuilding.

### Three Timeline Items

Created three Timeline Items demonstrating three of the four legal layers:

- **[[Story-Time-Three-Weeks]]** — `lfw_timeline_layer: story-time`. The April 4–25, 2026 story-time chronology in Item form (in addition to `_continuity.md`'s embedded timeline). The cartridge is small enough that the duplication is acceptable; for larger cartridges the Item would be source-of-truth
- **[[Family-History-1968-2026]]** — `lfw_timeline_layer: world-history`. Fifty-eight years of Hollis vineyard history; the world the novel sits inside. Demonstrates that for contemporary realism, "world-history" is the family/property history rather than cosmological backstory
- **[[Maya-Life-1984-2026]]** — `lfw_timeline_layer: character-specific`. Maya's life from birth to the novel's opening. Synchronized with the chronological backstory in [[Maya-Hollis-Bible]] but kept here in Item-form for Scene-level reference

The `real-world` layer is not used in this cartridge (it would be relevant for historical fiction).

Sarah's character-specific Timeline is deferred along with her bible.

### `_style-sheet.md`

Created `_style-sheet.md` with sections for spelling, capitalization, italics, punctuation, numbers, dialogue formatting, lexicon, and anachronism risk catalog (minimal — contemporary setting). The lexicon section has 14 entries including pronunciations for character names and viticultural terms.

The dialogue-formatting section cross-references chapter 13 §1 and reaffirms the choices already implicit in the Character Items' dialogue-tells.

### `_relationships.md`

Each pair's asymmetry between the two one-sided views is named explicitly. This is the worked example for F50 prevention: documenting intentional asymmetry so CHARACTER-CONSISTENCY can distinguish *intended difference* from *drift*.

## What I (AI) flagged during the session

- **Worldbuilding-as-procrastination protection.** When the writer initially expressed worry about under-using `_worldbuilding.md`, I named F45 (worldbuilding-as-procrastination) and recommended the minimal-stub approach rather than expanding the file to fill space. The minimal stub IS the right v1.3.2 demonstration for a contemporary-realism cartridge — sometimes the right artifact for a project is a deliberate one-page non-use
- **The Patchett Inspiration Item's lfw_for specificity.** When the writer first drafted the inspiration entry, the `lfw_for` field said "tone." I pushed for more specificity; the writer revised to *"inheritance + sibling structure; close-third POV with retrospective frame."* The specificity is the discipline — Inspiration Items with generic `lfw_for` fields are a tell that the inspiration hasn't been examined
- **Three Timeline Items — is that the right number?** The writer asked. My answer: for this cartridge, yes. Story-time + family-history + Maya-life cover the three perspectives the novel will most often draw on. Sarah-life is deferred; the deferral is consistent with the Sarah-bible deferral from v1.3.1 worked-example
- **Stakes-ladder's row-by-row discipline.** Drafting the ladder forced explicit declaration of when Societal stakes engage (Chapter 3; the vineyard's economic survival) and when Existential stakes engage (Chapter 6; the relationship's existence). The writer noted that the explicit declaration revealed Chapter 5 as currently *missing* a Personal-stakes texture for Sarah — she's the POV of even chapters, so Chapter 5 is Maya, but the writer registered the absence and added an open thread for the Chapter 5 outlining session
