# The Late Frost — Causal Spine

> **The plot's causal backbone. Premise as causal claim, dramatic question, scene-by-scene value-shifts, but/therefore audit. SCENE-AUDIT (chapter 11 §1) and READ-THROUGH activities work against this file.**

## Premise as causal claim

**Maya returns to the vineyard after three years of estrangement, *because* their father has died and the property has been left jointly to her and Sarah, *and* a forecasted late-frost season threatens to destroy the harvest — *therefore* the sisters must work together for three weeks despite the estrangement, *therefore* the debt-secret Sarah has been carrying for three months cannot remain hidden indefinitely, *therefore* one of them must speak first.**

*(Not: "Two sisters inherit a vineyard during a frost season." That's a setting and a situation. The causal claim is what makes it a plot.)*

## The dramatic question

**Will Sarah tell Maya about the debts before the harvest is settled, or will Maya find out from the letter — and will the relationship survive whichever it is?**

The question is asked by Chapter 1's planting (the letter on the kitchen counter; the unspoken weight) and answered by Chapter 13 (one sister speaks; the answer is shown by the next morning's interaction).

## Inevitability + surprise test

- **Inevitability:** The reader looking back from Chapter 14 should be able to trace every major turn to seeds planted in earlier chapters. Sarah's decision to speak in Chapter 13 is set up by Chapters 6, 8, 10 (each escalating Sarah's interior pressure). Maya's discovery in Chapter 5 is set up by Chapters 1, 3 (the moving letter; the empty house).
- **Surprise:** The form the relationship takes at the end should not be the form the reader expected at Chapter 1. Specifically: the reader will expect the relationship to be either repaired or shattered. The actual end-state is *neither* — the sisters have become honest with each other for the first time in twenty years, and the honesty has shown them they are not the people each thought the other was. That's harder than reconciliation and harder than estrangement.

## Escalation curve

The value-shift discipline must escalate stakes across the book, not run flat.

- **Chapter 1–2 (opening):** *Personal* stakes (Maya's interior unease; Sarah's interior secret-keeping). Small but real.
- **Chapter 3–5 (rising):** *Relational* stakes (the secret becomes visible; the trust becomes contingent).
- **Chapter 6–7 (Act Two middle):** *Confrontation-and-recoil* (the confrontation lands; the relationship has to choose to continue working under it).
- **Chapter 8–10 (Act Two crisis):** *Existential* stakes for the relationship (the buyer makes the option of escape real; the fight about the father makes the past unavoidable).
- **Chapter 11–14 (Act Three):** *Resolution at the level of who-each-sister-now-is* (the frost ends; the financial decision lands; the relationship reorganizes into the form that survives the truth).

### Inverted-pyramid check

Existential stakes don't enter until Chapter 6 (the relationship's existence) and the per-character identity-level doesn't engage until Chapter 10. The structure leaves room for escalation through the climax in Chapter 13. **No inverted-pyramid risk.**

### Flat-stakes check

The ladder above does not run flat at any level for any sustained stretch. Each chapter's row differs from the prior chapter's row by at least one cell. **No flat-stakes flag.**

The literary-subgenre tuning (per [[_manuscript-manifest]]'s `lfw_fiction_subgenre: literary`) accepts that *Societal* stakes are intermittent and *Existential* stakes are reserved for the back half. This is correct for the genre.

## Mid-act crisis and climax markers

- **Chapter 7 (Act Two middle):** The morning-after-the-confrontation is the structural pivot — the sisters work together under the new condition (the secret is now shared and contested). If this scene fails, the rest of Act Two has no foundation.
- **Chapter 10 (Act Two crisis):** The fight about the father. This is the lowest point. Sarah and Maya are further apart at the end of Chapter 10 than at the start of Chapter 1.
- **Chapter 13 (Act Three climax):** One sister speaks. The novel's load-bearing question is answered here. The decision-making weight of every earlier scene is paid off (or not) at Chapter 13's central moment.

## Honest open

The ending is structurally open on three of the major questions and closed on one. Closed: Sarah and Maya now know each other's actual relationship to the father and to each other (the closed question is: *who do these sisters now know themselves to be?*). Open: whether the vineyard is sold; whether the harvest is fully recovered; whether they will work together again next year. The open questions are the kind the form earns by closing the central question — the reader does not need them answered.
