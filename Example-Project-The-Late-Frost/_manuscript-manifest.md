# The Late Frost — Manuscript Manifest

> **Worked-example cartridge shipped with Long-Form-Writing v1.2 to demonstrate the fiction development layer. The manuscript is hypothetical — not a real published work. Treat the contents as illustrative of how a fiction cartridge looks at the early-drafting stage.**

## What this manuscript is

A literary novel about two estranged sisters who inherit their late father's struggling Sonoma vineyard during a late-frost season that may destroy the year's harvest — and who discover, over the three weeks of frost-watch, that the father left debts neither sister knew about. One sister has known for months; the other learns it now.

The frost is the external stake; the debt-secret is the relationship stake; the vineyard is the inheritance both sisters thought they understood and didn't.

Target length: 75,000 words. Literary fiction shape; close-third POV alternating between the two sisters.

## Premise / Logline

**Logline:** Two estranged sisters inherit a struggling family vineyard during a late-frost crisis; over the three weeks it takes to learn whether the harvest will survive, the older sister must decide whether to tell her younger sister about the debts that will force them to sell — knowing the telling will end the relationship she's spent twenty years trying to keep.

## Why this manuscript, why now

The writer is interested in how grief reorganizes adult sibling relationships under economic pressure. The vineyard / frost / debt structure provides external stakes that compress the relationship-decision into three weeks. The novel asks whether honesty can preserve a relationship that's been preserved by withholding.

## Intended audience

Literary-fiction readers. Comparable to *The Vanishing Half* (Bennett), *The Dutch House* (Patchett), *Hamnet* (O'Farrell). Readers who want family-secret tension at the literary-prose level, not thriller pacing.

## Comparable works

- Brit Bennett, *The Vanishing Half* (2020) — sister-relationship + family-secret architecture
- Ann Patchett, *The Dutch House* (2019) — inheritance + sibling dynamics
- Maggie O'Farrell, *Hamnet* (2020) — compressed time-period; high literary prose
- Tessa Hadley, *Free Love* (2022) — close-third literary fiction with multi-character POV

## Voice notes

The writer is in `writer-maintains` mode. No voice samples provided. The AI's job: structural, character-development, continuity, promise-tracking, reader-simulation. Not drafting prose.

Voice notes (writer's own):
- Close-third, present-tense for emotional immediacy
- Plain sentences for the sister-dialogue (the sisters talk like they grew up together); more layered sentences for narrative interiority
- Sensory detail load-bearing for the vineyard setting — wine, weather, vines, smoke
- Resist Hadley-influenced narrative distance; closer than that

## Cadence

- Target: 5 days per week, 60 min per session
- Typical: morning writing
- Expected horizon: 12–15 months from outline to manuscript ready for an editor

## Scope boundaries

- Not a thriller. The frost is real and the debt is real but the pacing is literary.
- Not a coming-of-age. The sisters are 41 and 38; this is mid-life.
- Not vineyard-procedural. The wine-making detail must be accurate but the book is not about how wine is made.
- Not a family-saga. The compressed time period (three weeks) is structural.

## Sensitivities

- Sibling relationships under grief — handle with care; the writer's own sister relationships informed the project
- Wildfire / fire imagery is adjacent to the vineyard but only as background; not a wildfire novel

## Communication preferences

- Register: peer
- Critique style: substantive
- Hedging: minimal
- Filler tolerance: none

## Genre-specific notes

- **POV:** Close-third, alternating Maya / Sarah by chapter. Maya gets odd chapters; Sarah gets even.
- **Tense:** Present.
- **Narrative distance:** Close. The `pov-and-psychic-distance` craft module is likely to be invoked during revision.
- **Worldbuilding:** Minimal. The contemporary California vineyard is recognizable; what needs continuity-tracking is the vineyard's specific mechanics + the family history.
